## A. Monthly cost at 1,000 users

Assumptions (all mine, stated once): 30-day month; 1,000 users × 10 docs × 20 KB = 200 MB live bytes; 30 saves/user/day = 900,000 saves/month; each save stores a full 20 KB immutable copy, so **new version bytes = 900,000 × 20 KB = 18 GB/month**, averaging 9 GB-month in month 1 and reaching 216 GB by the end of month 12; uploads 100 MB × 1,000 = 100 GB, all present from month 1; per save: 1 object write, 3 object reads, 2 record writes (version row + document head), 3 record reads, 0.5 KB per version record (450 MB/month of records); 200 live sessions/day = 6,000/month, each 60 min, 2 people, 10 incoming messages/min each (1,200 incoming, billed at 20:1 = 60 requests, plus 2 connection requests); DO memory 0.128 GB; "hibernating" means the object is active 10 % of the hour (360 s), "non-hibernating" means 3,600 s; Next.js on Vercel does 2 function invocations per save (1.8M/month) at 50 ms active CPU and 0.3 GB-s memory each; 40 KB transferred per invocation (72 GB/month). All prices exclude GST.

**(i) Planned stack (R2 + Firestore Mumbai + Firebase Auth + DO + Vercel Pro)**
- R2 month 1: (100 + 9 − 10 free) = 99 GB-month × $0.015 = **$1.49**; month 12: (100 + 207 − 10) × 0.015 = $4.46. Ops: 900k Class A < 1M free, 2.7M Class B < 10M free = $0.
- Firestore: writes 1.8M − 600k free (20,000 × 30) = 1.2M → 12 × $0.099 = $1.19; reads 2.7M − 1.5M free = 1.2M → 12 × $0.033 = $0.40; storage 0.45 GB < 1 GiB = $0 (month 12: 5.4 GB ≈ 5.03 GiB − 1 = 4.03 × $0.165 = $0.67). Month 1: **$1.58**. Requires Blaze, hence a billing account.
- Firebase Auth: 1,000 MAU ≪ 50K = **$0**.
- Durable Objects, hibernating: 372,000 requests/month = 12,400/day < 100,000; 6,000 × 46.08 GB-s = 276,480 GB-s = 9,216/day < 13,000 → **$0 on Workers Free**. Non-hibernating: 92,160 GB-s/day exceeds the free 13,000, so the Paid plan is forced: 2,764,800 − 400,000 = 2,364,800, rounded up to 3,000,000 × $12.50 = **$37.50 + $5 = $42.50**.
- Vercel Pro: seats $20 × 2 developers = **$40**. Usage: invocations (1.8M − 1M) × $0.60 = $0.48; Active CPU 25 h − 4 = 21 × $0.128 = $2.69; memory 150 GB-hrs < 360; transfer 72 GB < 1 TB. $3.17 total, inside the $20 credit → $0 extra.
- **Total: $43.07 with hibernation (one seat: $23.07); $85.57 without.**

## B. First bill and at what user count

- **Vercel is the first bill on every stack, from user one:** a Razorpay-charging product is commercial, and Hobby "restricts users to non-commercial, personal use only." First bill: $20 per developer seat per month.
- Planned stack beyond Vercel: R2 free 10 GB ÷ 100 MB per user = **100 users**, first R2 line 1 GB × $0.015 = $0.02 (rounded up per Cloudflare's rule). Firestore free writes 20,000/day ÷ 60 writes per user-day = **333 users**, first bill cents, but it needs Blaze from that point. Firebase Auth stays free to 50,000 MAU.
- Supabase-only: free file storage 1 GB ÷ 100 MB = **10 users**; the pause-after-one-week rule makes Free unfit for a product from day one; free DB 500 MB ÷ 0.45 MB per user-month ≈ 1,111 user-months. First bill **$25**.
- Cloudflare-only: R2 at 100 users ($0.02); D1 writes 100,000/day ÷ 60 = 1,667 users; Workers 100,000/day ÷ 60 = 1,667 users; DO 100,000 requests/day ÷ 62 = 1,613 sessions/day, and hibernating duration 13,000 ÷ 46.08 = 282 sessions/day, versus 13,000 ÷ 460.8 = **28 sessions/day without hibernation**. First non-R2 bill: Workers Paid $5.

## C. Stated ceilings

- **Firestore**: 1 MiB per document; 10 MiB per API request; one free database per project; 500 writes/s on a collection with a sequential indexed field; Mumbai storage $0.165/GiB-month, 11× R2. Documents over 1 MiB cannot live in Firestore records at all.
- **R2**: 5 TiB per object, 5 GiB single-part upload, 1 write/s per key (immutable per-version keys avoid this); APAC hint only, no India pin.
- **D1**: 10 GB per database "cannot be further increased"; 500 MB on Free; 2 MB row; single-threaded, "1 ms query ≈ 1,000 qps".
- **Durable Objects**: 10 GB storage per object, 2 MB key+value, 1,000 rps soft limit per object, 128 MB memory; Free plan fails hard when a daily limit is exceeded.
- **Workers**: 100 MB request body on Free/Pro zones, so 100 MB uploads must go straight to R2 (presigned), not through a Worker.
- **Supabase**: Free 500 MB DB, 1 GB files, 50 MB per file, 200 Realtime connections, 2M messages, pause after 1 week; Pro 8 GB disk then $0.125/GB, Micro 60 direct / 200 pooled connections, 500 GB per file, 500 connections, 5M messages; Mumbai region exists.
- **Neon**: 0.5 GB per free project; no India region (Singapore nearest). **Turso**: 5 GB, 10M row writes/month free (1.8M fits). **PlanetScale**: nothing free.
- **Vercel**: Hobby non-commercial; Pro CDN 1M requests and 1 TB flat; Blob 5 TB per file.
- **Firebase Storage**: Blaze only, and no-cost quota only in three US regions.

## D. Verdict

R2 is the right home for the bytes on every stack: $0.015/GB-month, free egress, no minimum, 5 TiB objects, and the 1,000-user workload never leaves its free operation tiers ($1.49/month in month 1). Firestore is the weak piece. At 1,000 users it costs about $1.58/month, so cost is not the objection; the objections are structural: it needs a Blaze billing account (and its own FAQ flags Indian cards as a known failure), its free quota resets at midnight Pacific rather than IST, its storage is 11× R2's, the 1 MiB document limit rules out ever storing content or large diffs in a record, and a credits ledger plus version history are relational, transactional data that the team's existing repos already model in Postgres. Supabase Pro at $25 buys a Mumbai Postgres with 100 GB of object storage (covering the 100 GB of uploads exactly), auth for 100,000 MAU, daily backups, and Realtime, for a total of about $65 versus $43 on the planned stack, a gap of $22/month that is the price of one database the team already knows instead of two services it does not. Its Free tier is unusable for this product: 1 GB of files is ten users' uploads, and a project that pauses after a week of inactivity is not a product. The cheapest stack by far is Cloudflare-only at $1.49 to $6.49, but it moves the Next.js app off Vercel (a migration this report did not evaluate) and D1's 10 GB hard ceiling per database means sharding by tenant from the start.

Recommendation from the numbers: **R2 for bytes, Supabase Pro (Mumbai) for records, ledger and auth, Durable Objects with the Hibernation API for live sessions, Vercel Pro for the app.** Hard constraints the plan must respect regardless of choice:

1. Vercel Hobby is non-commercial; budget $20 per developer seat from day one.
2. Use the DO WebSocket Hibernation API; without it, live sessions are the largest line on the bill ($42.50 at 200 sessions/day) and the free plan fails at 28 sessions/day.
3. Firestore's 1 MiB document limit and Blaze requirement, if Firestore stays.
4. Supabase Free pauses after one week; Pro is the floor.
5. 100 MB uploads must bypass Workers (100 MB body cap) and go direct to R2.
6. Version bytes grow 18 GB/month per 1,000 users at 30 full-copy saves/day; storing deltas or deduplicating would cut the only cost line that compounds.
