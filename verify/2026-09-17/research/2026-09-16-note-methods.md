# Nine note-taking methods and what each asks of an editor

I opened every page with curl on 2026-09-16, and all quotes are verbatim from the linked page. Search and WebFetch were used only to find pages, never as a source of quotes. No page failed to open. The Obsidian material comes from its help-site source files on GitHub.

On the claims to verify:
- **Matuschak's wording is exact.** "Most people use notes as a bucket for storage or scratch thoughts" appears verbatim as a linked note title inside his note "Most people take only transient notes".
- **Ink & Switch URL.** `inkandswitch.com/local-first/` redirects to `/essay/local-first/`.

## 1. Zettelkasten (zettelkasten.de, by "Sascha")
**What it is:** "A Zettelkasten is a personal tool for thinking and writing. It has hypertextual features to make a web of thoughts possible." ([intro](https://zettelkasten.de/introduction/))

**What it asks of a tool:**
- **Fixed addresses:** "The fixed address of each note is the alpha and omega of the world of Zettelkasten." A time-based ID "never changes, so you can change the title as much as you want without breaking any links."
- **One idea per note:** "aiming for one knowledge building block per note."
- **Structure notes and navigation:** a structure note is "a Zettel about other Zettels and their relationships". Navigation relies on "Full-text search and being able to follow links", plus "A kind of sandbox".
- **Links that say why they exist:** "If you just add links without any explanation you will not create knowledge."

**What it says tools get wrong:**
- **Lock-in:** "training the user to depend on features that are not available anywhere else."
- **Title links that update on rename:** the title "nudges you to write the note so that the note content adapts to the title of the linked note". The fix is to "separate the ID and the title" ([2026 post](https://zettelkasten.de/posts/the-hidden-problem-with-note-titles-as-links-and-how-to-fix-it/)).
- **Automatic backlinks:** "a feature of a program seems to be useful but in reality distracts from that what you actually want to do" ([2020 post](https://zettelkasten.de/posts/backlinks-are-bad-links/)).

## 2. Evergreen notes (Andy Matuschak)
**What it is:** "Evergreen notes are written and organized to evolve, contribute, and accumulate over time, across projects." ([page](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Evergreen_notes))

**Why most note-taking fails:** the "bucket" note title quoted above sits inside ["Most people take only transient notes"](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zKGjQtsTKgscAoq271ZzKqw). That note warns of "a pile of dissociated notes."

**What it asks of a tool:**
- **Atomic notes:** "only about one thing—but which, as much as possible, capture the entirety of that thing" ([note](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zNUaiGAXp21eorsER1Jm9yU)).
- **Notes organised by concept:** "factor Evergreen notes by concept (rather than by author, book, event, project, topic, etc)" ([note](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/z2hQEhqWkdRLL9JUwfawZZx)).
- **Dense linking, including to notes not yet written:** "it's very freeing to be able to link to a stub" ([note](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zF8xCU4BwXwbmSyp7tmff9i)). One linked title reads "Backlinks can be used to implicitly define nodes in knowledge management systems."
- **A writing inbox:** "a quick way to capture transient notes which clearly isolates them from evergreen notes" ([note](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zUP4GuzPF33dWkZPiu9N6V5)).

**What he says tools get wrong:**
- **Tags** produce "a jumbled, unordered list" ([note](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zojJRcfGstU2Ss6JRMzd15)).
- **Hierarchy imposed early** means "you prematurely constrain what may emerge" ([note](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/z8SU3r8xyZyvwRhyDdJasJ2)).
- **The framing itself:** "what matters is not “computer-support note-taking” but “computer supported thinking.”" ([note](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zAf4oNSV9qB38ncSvYEZGAb))

His ["Tools for thought"](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Tools_for_thought) note is only a short hub: where the term came from (Iverson, 1980) and links about funding. It contains no critique of tools.

## 3. PARA, CODE and progressive summarisation (Tiago Forte)
**What it is:**
- **PARA** is "a simple, comprehensive, yet extremely flexible system for organizing any type of digital information across any platform" ([PARA](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/)).
- **CODE** "stands for Capture, Organize, Distill, and Express" ([guide](https://fortelabs.com/blog/basboverview/)).
- **Progressive summarisation** is "a method for opportunistic compression" ([PS](https://fortelabs.com/blog/progressive-summarization-a-practical-technique-for-designing-discoverable-notes/)).

**What it asks of a tool:**
- **Four top-level folders**, organised "according to the projects and goals you are committed to right now."
- **An inbox:** "a separate folder as a temporary holding area where new items accumulate", emptied in a weekly batch (guide).
- **Summary layers:** bold "only the best parts", then "I switch to highlighting", then a summary "at the top of the note, restating the key points in my own words."
- **One place for everything:** capture into "a single, centralized place", with notes "resurfaced using RandomNote" (PS).

**What he says tools get wrong** (all from PS):
- "relying on tagging is too fragile and requires too much maintenance".
- "notebooks and folders actually suppress the serendipity and randomness".
- Notes designed around their content, rather than around folders or tags, "can more easily survive migrations to other devices, storage locations, and even programs".

## 4. Linking Your Thinking and Maps of Content (Nick Milo)
**What it is:** "Maps of Content are a special type of note that supercharges how fast you can build relationships between ideas." ([Maps](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/maps/)) The linkingyourthinking.com home page mentions MOCs only inside testimonials.

**What it asks of a tool:**
- **Three jobs:** "MOCs help you do three things: gather, develop, and navigate your ideas."
- **Overlays, not containers:** MOCs are "“overlays” that add relevant information but that don’t affect your base level notes" ([overview](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/mocs-overview)).
- **A home note:** "you will want a home note" ([Ideaverse Map](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/ideaverse-map)).
- **Spatial arrangement:** "Relational Positioning".

**What he says tools get wrong:**
- "In link-based thinking tools (like Obsidian), an MOC often gets mistaken as a boring index" ([defn](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/mocs-(defn))).
- **Folders:** unlike folders, MOCs "are nondestructive, non-restrictive, non-limiting perspectives" (overview).
- **Hand-kept lists:** "A-Z manual lists are usually a waste of time because they will just become outdated" ([note](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/mocs-encourage-flexible-non-destructive-thinking)).

## 5. File over app (Steph Ango; the page says he is Obsidian's CEO)
**What it is:** "if you want to create digital artifacts that last, they must be files you can control, in formats that are easy to retrieve and read." ([page](https://stephango.com/file-over-app), 1 July 2023)

**What it asks of tool makers:** "accept that all software is ephemeral, and give people ownership over their data."

**What he says tools get wrong:** "Even the files on your hard drive use proprietary formats that make them incompatible with older systems and other tools." He applies this to Obsidian itself: "it’s a delusion to think it will last forever."

## 6. Local-first software (Kleppmann, Wiggins, van Hardenberg, McGranaghan; April 2019)
**What it is:** "a set of principles for software that enables both collaboration and ownership for users." ([essay](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/local-first/))

**The seven ideals (verbatim headings):**
1. No spinners: your work at your fingertips
2. Your work is not trapped on one device
3. The network is optional
4. Seamless collaboration with your colleagues
5. The Long Now
6. Security and privacy by default
7. You retain ultimate ownership and control

**What they say cloud apps get wrong:** "If a service shuts down, the software stops functioning, and data created with that software is lost." Also, "cloud apps typically don’t work while offline."

**On plain files:** files "can be viewed and edited offline, they give full control to users". However, "accessing files from multiple devices is trickier."

## 7. Daily notes, interstitial journaling and inboxes
- **Obsidian daily notes:** "Daily notes is a core plugin that opens a note based on today's date, or creates it if it doesn't exist". A template is optional ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-help/master/en/Plugins/Daily%20notes.md)).
- **Interstitial journaling** ([Ness Labs](https://nesslabs.com/interstitial-journaling); by Anne-Laure Le Cunff, 15 Apr 2020; the technique was "created by Tony Stubblebine"). You "write a few lines every time you take a break, and to track the exact time you are taking these notes." The page adds, "You don’t need any special software."
- **Inboxes:** Matuschak's linked note titles warn "Inboxes only work if you trust how they’re drained". Another says Inbox Zero-style triage is "often too brittle".
- **What these ask of a tool:** a one-key "today" note, a timestamp command, tasks inside the note, and an inbox with a routine for emptying it.

## 8. Spaced repetition and tools for thought (Matuschak and Nielsen, October 2019)
**What it is:** tools for thought are tools "to augment human intelligence". The "mnemonic medium" is their name for reading material with review cards built in; it "makes it almost effortless for users to remember what they read", using "increasing time intervals between reviews of particular questions" ([essay](https://numinous.productions/ttft/)).

**What it asks of a tool:** cards written inside the reading and writing; card writing treated as a skill ("card-writing is better thought of as an open-ended skill"); scheduled review.

**What they say tools get wrong:**
- "Tools for thought are (mostly) public goods, and as a result are undersupplied".
- "much of the work has been stuck in Spock-space".
- Anki and SuperMemo "seem to most often be used for similar simple declarative knowledge".

**A related Matuschak note:** maintaining evergreen notes "isn’t an efficient spaced repetition, memory-wise" ([note](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zWbMsEFW9LD4vsoVhaDcF4u)).

## 9. Johnny.Decimal
**What it is:** "Johnny.Decimal is a system that helps you be more organised. It gives you a simple structure so you can easily find your stuff." ([home](https://johnnydecimal.com/)) Also: "You assign a unique ID to everything in your life." ([intro](https://johnnydecimal.com/documentation/introduction))

**What it asks of a tool:**
- **A three-level numbering scheme:** areas (`10-19`), categories (`15`) and IDs (`15.22`). The docs advise "Prefer fewer, broader categories".
- **An index:** a JDex is "the master record of every ID in a system", kept as "One note per ID" ([JDex](https://johnnydecimal.com/documentation/the-jdex)).
- **Quick search:** "Any app that lets you search your notes quickly will work".
- **Metadata under the title:** Description, Location, Relates to and Keywords ([notes](https://johnnydecimal.com/documentation/keeping-notes)).

**What it says tools get wrong:** on nested folders, "It would be chaos. But this is how you save computer files." Also: "Your filesystem is not your index" and "Files are heavy from a mental perspective."

## A. Requirements, which methods need them, and the feature that serves them
Abbreviations: ZK Zettelkasten, EG Evergreen, PARA (includes CODE and progressive summarisation), LYT, FoA File over app, LF Local-first, DN Daily/interstitial/inbox, SR Spaced repetition, JD Johnny.Decimal.

| Requirement | Needed by | Editor feature |
|---|---|---|
| Portable plain files | ZK, FoA, LF, JD, PARA | `.md` on disk; open any folder |
| Fast capture | EG, PARA, DN, JD (LF: "No spinners") | Quick-capture command, web clipper, instant launch |
| Atomic notes | ZK, EG, PARA ("individual atoms"), SR (cards) | Extract selection to a new note; split and merge |
| Links between notes | ZK, EG, LYT; optional in JD ("Relates to") and DN | `[[` autocomplete; links to notes not yet written |
| Full-text search | ZK, JD, PARA | Instant search; prefix filter such as `15.` |
| Maps of content, index, entry points | ZK, LYT, JD | Home and structure notes; generated lists |
| Resurfacing and review | PARA, EG, SR | Random note; review queue |
| An inbox | EG, PARA, DN (to-read lists) | Inbox folder or tag; "process inbox" view |
| Shallow fixed top level | PARA, JD, LYT ("In just 3 folders") | Folder scaffolds; one-step archive |
| Stable links across renames | ZK, JD ("I never re-use IDs") | Link by ID, or rewrite links on rename; alias text |
| Unique note IDs | ZK, JD | Timestamp ID; "next free ID" |
| Link context | ZK, EG ("Prefer labeled associations") | Backlinks shown with their paragraph |
| Backlinks | EG for; ZK against automatic ones | Optional backlinks panel |
| Templates | ZK (fixed note anatomy), JD (metadata), DN | Templates with date and title variables |
| Daily notes | DN, EG ("Daily working log") | "Open today" command |
| Tags and properties | JD, ZK (tags as search strings); EG and PARA warn against tags as the main structure | YAML properties; `#tag` search |
| Canvas | ZK ("sandbox"), LYT | Spatial board of notes |
| Progressive highlighting | PARA | Bold, then highlight, then summary; a highlights-only view |
| Block references | EG ("Prefer fine-grained associations"); ZK only when notes are not atomic | `^id` anchors |
| Transclusion | None require it; EG mentions it once | Live embeds |
| Spaced repetition | SR (EG says maintenance only approximates it) | Card syntax and a scheduler |
| Works offline and across devices | Offline: LF, FoA. Devices: LF, PARA | Local-first storage with sync |
| Search by meaning | None of the pages require it | Optional embedding-based search |

## B. What plain Markdown lacks, and what Obsidian does natively versus through plugins
**What plain Markdown defines.** I searched the terms in the [CommonMark 0.31.2 spec](https://spec.commonmark.org/0.31.2/) and the [GFM spec](https://github.github.com/gfm/). Neither has any hit for highlight syntax, front matter/YAML, transclusion or backlinks, and their `[[` hits are nested-bracket examples, not a link syntax. GFM adds task lists and strikethrough. Obsidian lists `[[Link]]`, `![[Link]]`, `^id` and `==Text==` under "Supported Markdown extensions" ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-help/master/en/Editing%20and%20formatting/Obsidian%20Flavored%20Markdown.md)).

**What the file format alone does not provide:** wikilinks, rename-safe links, backlinks, block references, transclusion, highlights, properties, templates, daily notes, generated IDs, generated MOC lists, spaced repetition, search by meaning, and inbox processing. Whether any particular plain editor adds some of these is not verified.

**What plain files do provide:** portability, offline editing, and linking done as searches. Zettelkasten.de says "you can reproduce your workflow with about any plain text editor in the world".

**Obsidian, natively** (core plugins listed on its [Core plugins page](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-help/master/en/Plugins/Core%20plugins.md) and the pages linked from it):
- **Backlinks**, including "Unlinked mentions".
- **Rename-safe links:** "Obsidian can automatically update internal links in your vault when you rename a file."
- **Block links and embeds:** embeds "stay up to date when the source file changes".
- **Highlights** (`==Text==`).
- **Other core plugins:** Templates, Daily notes, Unique note creator ("time-based names, also known as Zettelkasten notes"), Random note, Canvas, Note composer ("Merge two notes or split one into two."), Properties view, Tags view, and Bases ("database-like views of your notes").
- **Web Clipper**, a separate official "free browser extension that lets you highlight pages and save web content to your vault."
- **Storage:** "Obsidian stores your notes as Markdown-formatted plain text files in a vault."

**Obsidian, only through community plugins:**
- **Spaced repetition,** for example `obsidian-spaced-repetition`: "Fight the forgetting curve by reviewing flashcards & entire notes." It is in the [plugin registry](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-releases/master/community-plugins.json), which had 7,693 entries when I fetched it.
- **Search by meaning.** Core Search works "by using search terms and operators" ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-help/master/en/Plugins/Search.md)). Community plugins fill the gap, for example `smart-lookup`: "find notes by meaning when exact words fail".
- **Caveat:** "By default, Obsidian runs in Restricted Mode to prevent third-party code execution." ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-help/master/en/Extending%20Obsidian/Plugin%20security.md))

**Not verified in Obsidian:** a dedicated inbox workflow (the Tags page only uses `#inbox/to-read` as an example), a highlights-only view, a "next free ID" helper, and mobile quick capture.

## C. The requirements shared by the most methods
The counts are tallies of the "Needed by" column in table A.
1. **Portable plain files:** 5 of 9 (ZK, FoA, LF, JD, PARA).
2. **Fast capture:** 4 of 9 (EG, PARA, DN, JD). Counting Ink & Switch's "No spinners" makes it 5.
3. **Atomic notes:** 4 of 9 (ZK, EG, PARA, SR).
4. **Links between notes:** 3 require them (ZK, EG, LYT); 5 counting optional use (JD, DN).
5. **Fifth place is a five-way tie at 3 of 9 each:**
   - full-text search (ZK, JD, PARA)
   - entry-point or index notes (ZK, LYT, JD)
   - resurfacing (PARA, EG, SR)
   - an inbox (EG, PARA, DN)
   - a shallow fixed top level (PARA, JD, LYT)

**Where the sources disagree** (worth making these settings, not defaults):
- **Automatic backlinks:** Matuschak favours them; zettelkasten.de calls them harmful.
- **Titles as link targets:** Matuschak says titles "become an abstraction for the note itself" ([note](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zDh1yhNFQNxDEre12B4zd8k)); zettelkasten.de says "separate the ID and the title".
- **Tags:** Matuschak and Forte warn against them as the main structure; Johnny.Decimal and zettelkasten.de use them as searchable strings.
