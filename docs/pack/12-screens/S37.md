## Purpose

Four switches that turn parts of the product on and off, and two rows that look like switches and
are not.

## Entry and exit

how a person arrives | route or control
A founder, from the panel nav | `/config/flags`
A screen that is currently off, when someone asks why | The same route, where the flag names the screens it governs

how they leave | what happens
Saving | The flag takes effect on the next read. No deploy
Discarding | The stored states return
The panel nav | Another section, with any pending change still pending

**The locked rows are shown, not hidden.** They are on the screen because the reason belongs beside
the value. A hidden constraint is a constraint someone will try to change by other means.

## Data contract

what | direction | port or store | shape | when
Flag states | read | `FeatureFlagReader.all()` | id, state, locked, the reason where locked | On opening, and by the feature gate on every read elsewhere
Flag state | write | `FeatureFlagWriter.set(id, state)` | one flag | On a switch
Locked rows | read | `FeatureFlagReader.locked()` | id, held value, reason | On opening
Audit row | write | `ConfigAuditWriter.record(change)` | who, which flag, from, to, when | In the same transaction as the write
Flag, everywhere else | read | `FeatureFlagReader.isOn(id)` | boolean | At the point of use, never cached past a request

**One gate, like the one read path for caps.** Section 30 lists feature flags as a setting read by
the feature gate. The same discipline as `limitsFor(account)` applies: a screen that decides its own
availability from a constant is a second source of truth, and the architecture gate is the place to
catch it.

**A locked row has no writer at all.** `FeatureFlagWriter` does not accept a locked id. That is a
type-level refusal rather than a runtime check, so the impossible state cannot be represented.

**`src/modules/config/` does not exist** at `0af3c90`, verified with `ls src/modules/`. Specified,
not built.

## Actions

action | trigger | what happens | failure behaviour | event id
Turn a flag on | The switch | The feature is available from the next read | `E370` when the write fails. The switch returns to its stored state | `config.flag.toggled`
Turn a flag off | The switch | The screens it governs stop being reachable, and say why rather than 404 | `E370` | `config.flag.toggled`
Touch a locked row | Clicking where a switch would be | Nothing happens. The reason is already on the row | `E371`, recorded so the attempt is visible in the audit | `config.flag.blocked`
Save | The pending bar | Every change is written and audited in one transaction | `E372` when a flag changed underneath. Nothing is written and the flag is named | `config.change.saved`
Change the age floor | Not available here | The number is displayed and not editable | `E373` if attempted by any path, naming consent as the reason | `config.flag.blocked`

**Turning a flag off is not the same as deleting a feature.** A person mid-session in a live
collaboration when live editing is turned off keeps their session to its end. Turning the flag off
stops new ones. `INFERENCE:` the plan does not say this, and the alternative, cutting a live session
at a founder's click, would be a data-loss shaped event. See `D98`.

## Keyboard and focus

- Focus lands on the first flag.
- Up and down move through the rows. Space operates a switch.
- **A locked row is focusable.** It is read aloud with its lock state and its reason, rather than
  being skipped, because a person who cannot see the dashed border has the most need of the reason.
- Tab leaves the list for the pending bar.
- **No shortcut saves.** A flag here turns a screen on for everyone.

## What this screen must never do

- **Never make the training promise a switch.** Section 30 is explicit: it is a claim about the
  provider list, and its truth is maintained on S36, not here.
- **Never let the age floor be edited.** The panel holds the number. Changing it re-opens consent for
  everyone who accepted the old terms, which is a process rather than a click.
- **Never hide the locked rows.** The reason belongs beside the value.
- **Never let a flag change be silent.** Four switches govern whole screens, and each change is
  audited with who, from what, to what and when.
- **Never let a screen decide its own availability.** One gate, read at the point of use.
- **Never turn a flag off in a way that takes something already in progress.** Off stops new, it does
  not cut running.
- **Never return a not-found for a feature that is merely off.** A person who followed a link to a
  turned-off screen is owed a sentence, not a dead end.
- **Never let this screen change a cap.** Caps are S35, through one read path.

## Open questions

id | question | why it is open
`D98` | What happens to work in progress when a flag goes off? | `INFERENCE:` a running live session finishes. Nothing in the plan says so, and the alternative would cut people off mid-sentence
`D99` | Is the indexing flag global, or per published page? | Section 10 says pages are not indexed today and that whether they should be is open. A global flag and a per-page choice are different products, and S17 would carry the per-page control
`D100` | Does turning off bring-your-own key revoke keys already stored? | Off should stop the field appearing. Whether stored keys stop being used is a different and larger question, and it touches money
`D101` | Should there be a fifth flag for the desktop's local model? | It is raised on S36 as `D97`. It is a capability that is on or off, which is this screen's shape

**What is not specified here.** The screens each flag governs are specified in their own files. The
sign-in page's wording is S01's. The consent process behind the age floor is section 23's legal
floor, not a screen. Nothing here was checked against a running gate, because none exists. What
would falsify this file: a decision that the training promise may be set per provider rather than
held as one claim, which would turn the locked row into a computed one and change what the sign-in
page can say.
