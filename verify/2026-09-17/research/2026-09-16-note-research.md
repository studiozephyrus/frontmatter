## A. Do people re-find by browsing or searching?

For material people organise themselves, browsing wins. Bergman et al. (2008): folders for 56 to 68% of file retrievals, search 4 to 15%, better search engines barely moved it (self-estimates). Teevan et al. (2004): people step towards targets they already know exactly. Barreau and Nardi (1995): location doubles as a reminder. Still true recently: in Bergman and Dvir (2024, Human-Computer Interaction, 1 cite, https://doi.org/10.1080/07370024.2024.2379838) a control group searched for 5% of retrievals, while heavy searchers had less organised files and failed at navigation 23% of the time against 6%.

For large streams other people create, it flips. Whittaker et al. (2011), logging 345 email users, found folders did not improve refinding while search and threading did; Teevan et al. (2007) put re-finding at 40% of web queries; Dumais et al. (2003) found time and people are strong cues. Notes are self-written, so the file evidence is the closer match, but that is my inference: nothing returned measured re-finding inside a note app.

## B. Are links between notes worth it?

Thin, and mostly indirect. The solid studies concern reading someone else's hypertext and are mixed: more effective but less efficient (Chen and Rada 1996), useful mainly for search-heavy tasks (Dillon and Gabbard 1998), disorienting (Botafogo et al. 1992), possibly harmful to learning when linking is heavy (Niederhauser et al. 2000). The best case for connecting ideas is Nesbit and Adesope (2006), but that is classroom concept mapping, not backlinks in a vault. The only direct study of a modern linked-notes tool returned is a single-lab Obsidian case study (Ferreira et al. 2025) with no outcome measure in its abstract. Nothing shows backlinks or a graph view improve re-finding, recall or thinking.

## C. Trusting and accepting AI suggestions

People accept too readily: Buçinca et al. (2021) found they take wrong AI advice, and the designs that fixed it most were liked least. Lee et al. (2025) found more confidence in GenAI went with less critical thinking. Gains are real on suitable tasks (Noy and Zhang 2023), but Dell'Acqua et al. (2026) found AI users 19% less likely to be correct outside the AI's frontier, which they describe as uneven across tasks of similar apparent difficulty. Suggestions steer content: an opinionated assistant shifted what people wrote and later believed (Jakesch et al. 2023, CHI, 243 cites, https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581196), and AI ideas made stories better individually but more alike (Doshi and Hauser 2024, Science Advances, 703 cites, https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290). People do not feel they own AI text, yet do not disclose it (Draxler et al. 2023). Lee and See (2004) set the goal: appropriate reliance, not maximum trust.

## D. Three product decisions

1. **Folder tree as the main route back to a note, kept shallow, with search as a strong fallback; tags not the organising model.** Bergman et al. 2008 and 2013, Teevan et al. 2004. Bergman et al. (2010, JASIST, 59 cites, https://doi.org/10.1002/asi.21415) found retrieval time and success depend on folder depth and size, and judged people's shallow, small folders adaptive.
2. **Capture without filing.** An unfiled inbox, no required title, folder or tag at capture, filing offered later. Malone 1983 (piles exist because categorising is hard), Whittaker and Hirschberg 2001 (filers kept more and used it less), Bernstein et al. 2008 (scraps need quick, free-form entry).
3. **Mark AI-written spans automatically and require an explicit accept for AI edits.** People will not declare AI text themselves (Draxler et al. 2023) and they over-accept suggestions (Buçinca et al. 2021). Test the friction: Buçinca found the most effective forcing designs were the least liked.

## E. Claims the evidence does not support

- **Saving notes makes you remember more.** Offloading hurt memory for offloaded material absent a goal to learn (Grinschgl et al. 2021); expected access shifted recall from content to location (Sparrow et al. 2011, with the caveat above). Saving can free memory for the next thing (Storm and Stone 2014). A note app is external memory, not a memory booster.
- **Linked notes or a graph view help you think or find things.** Untested for personal notes; the hypertext work shows efficiency costs and disorientation.
- **Good search means you never need to organise.** Contradicted for self-organised files (Bergman et al. 2008), though true for email (Whittaker et al. 2011).
- **Tags beat folders.** Bergman et al. (2013) found the opposite for storage and retrieval.
