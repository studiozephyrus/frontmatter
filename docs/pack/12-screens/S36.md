## Purpose

Which providers we are allowed to use, in what order, and which model serves which call on which
plan.

**The routing the generator draws.** Three call kinds: an edit, a document, a blueprint. Each names
its model on Free and on Pro and the cost of one call. Section 14 of the plan has the same three and
adds a fourth, the desktop, which runs a local model with nothing leaving the machine.

**Room for ten to thirteen models, not three.**

**A founders' instruction, given directly.** `[Z]`
`docs/mvp0/SCREEN-CHANGES-2026-09-18.md:214`: S36 needs more model layouts, ten to thirteen models,
when it is built. The drawn screen has three routing rows and five provider rows, and that is the
gap.

**The count, derived at write time rather than carried.** Taken from section 14's provider table:

```text
Groq                  gpt-oss-120b, gpt-oss-20b, qwen3.8-27b, compound
Cloudflare Workers AI qwen3-30b
Cerebras              gpt-oss-120b, qwen-3.8-27b
SambaNova             DeepSeek-V3.1, Llama 3.3 70B, gpt-oss-120b

free chain provider-model rows: 10
distinct cloud model names, free plus Pro: 10
plus four local models: 14 distinct in total
```

**So the plan already names ten cloud models and fourteen in total.** The founders' ten to thirteen
is not an aspiration to fill. It is the size the screen has to be to show what the plan already
holds. Three rows cannot.

**What that means for the layout, concretely.**

- The routing table gains a **model axis**, not just a call axis. A cell becomes a choice among the
  models a provider actually serves, rather than a single name typed in.
- A provider row **expands to its models**, each with its own switch, because Groq's four are not
  interchangeable and SambaNova's limit is 20 requests a day **per model**, section 14.
- The desktop's local models are a fifth block, not a footnote. Four of them fit an 8 GB machine and
  they are the only ones that cost nothing per use.
- **The cheapest layout that holds fourteen rows is a table, not a set of cards.** The generator
  already uses `.cfgt` for the routing and `.cfgprov` cards for the chain, and the cards are what
  does not scale.

## Data contract

what | direction | port or store | shape | when
Provider list | read | `ProviderConfigReader.all()` | name, order, enabled, terms-opened, the models it serves | On opening
Terms record | read | `ProviderConfigReader.terms(provider)` | who opened the terms, the URL, the date, and the training clause quoted | On opening, and before any enable
Pool state | read | `ProviderLedgerReader.state()` | what is left today per provider, **and how that was known** | On opening, and never by a probe call
Routing | read and write | `RoutingConfigReader.all()`, `RoutingConfigWriter.save()` | call kind, plan, provider, model | On opening, and on save
Chain order | write | `ProviderConfigWriter.reorder(order)` | the list, in order | On dropping a dragged row
Enable or disable | write | `ProviderConfigWriter.setEnabled(provider, on)` | one provider | On the switch
Cost of one call | read | `CostReader.perCall(callKind, model)` | a computed figure | On render
Audit row | write | `ConfigAuditWriter.record(change)` | who, which setting, from, to, when | In the same transaction as every write above

**The cost cell is computed, never typed.** Section 14 gives the token shape of each call kind and
the per-million price of each model. Multiplying them is arithmetic the screen can do, and a typed
figure is the exact defect the pack's own rule against carrying numbers forward exists to stop.

**The remaining-pool cell must say how it was known**, because for two of the four providers it
cannot be known at all. `[M]` from the router research, opened 2026-09-18:

provider | can the remaining pool be read before spending it?
Groq | Requests for the day, yes, from a header. **Daily tokens, no**, and that is the limit that bites. `docs/research/2026-09-18-llm/raw/L3-router-design.md:623`
Cloudflare Workers AI | No. Exhaustion is known only from a 429 carrying internal code `3036`. `docs/research/2026-09-18-llm/raw/L3-router-design.md:594`
Cerebras | No, and it has no reset either, because its quota refills continuously. `docs/research/2026-09-18-llm/raw/L3-router-design.md:636`
OpenRouter | Yes, from its key endpoint, and it is the only one in the survey that will say

**So the column is our ledger's count, not the provider's.** A cell that presents our own running
total as the provider's authority would be the most quietly wrong number in the panel.

**`src/modules/ai/` exists with `application` and `infrastructure` and no presentation layer**, and
`src/modules/config/` does not exist at all. `[O]` verified with `ls src/modules/ai/` and
`ls src/modules/` at `0af3c90`. Specified, not built.

## Actions

action | trigger | what happens | failure behaviour | event id
Reorder the chain | Dragging a row | The order is stored and the next call follows it | `E106` when the order cannot be written. The rows return to the stored order | `config.provider.reordered`
Enable a provider | The switch | The provider joins the chain at its position | `E090` when the terms have not been recorded. **The switch does not operate at all** | `config.provider.toggled`
Disable a provider | The switch | It leaves the chain. Calls route past it | None. Disabling is always allowed | `config.provider.toggled`
Try to enable an unread provider | Clicking a disabled switch | Nothing happens, and the row already says why | `E062`, recorded so the attempt is visible in the audit | `config.provider.blocked`
Record terms | Pasting the URL, the date and the training clause | The row becomes enableable | `E046` when the clause is empty. A recorded blank is worse than no record | `config.provider.terms.recorded`
Change a routing cell | Picking a model | That call kind uses that model on that plan, from the next call | `E089` when the model is not served by an enabled provider | `config.setting.changed`
Save | The pending bar | Every change is written and audited in one transaction | `E119` when a row changed underneath. Nothing is written and the row is named | `config.change.saved`

**The disabled row is question 3 made mechanical.** Section 5b: a provider whose terms nobody has
opened cannot be switched on, and the control is disabled and says so on the row. Section 30 adds
the reason a switch cannot carry it: the training promise is a claim about which providers are in
the chain, so adding one whose terms permit training changes the sentence on the sign-in page, not a
row in a table.

## Keyboard and focus

- Focus lands on the first provider row.
- Up and down move through the chain. A modifier with up or down **reorders**, so the chain is
  changeable without a drag, which a drag-only list would deny to a keyboard user entirely.
- Space operates a switch. A switch that does not operate says why on focus, rather than being
  silently inert.
- Tab leaves the chain for the routing table, which is navigated like S35's table.
- **No shortcut saves.** A save here changes where every document in the product is sent.

## Responsive

- **The phone shows the chain, read-only**, each provider with its remaining pool. The generator
  draws exactly that and states that editing is on the desktop.
- The routing table is not shown on the phone. With ten to fourteen models it cannot be, and a
  truncated routing table would be worse than none.
- `[Z]` the phone view takes the same theme treatment as the desktop,
  `docs/mvp0/SCREEN-CHANGES-2026-09-18.md:19`.

## What this screen must never do

- **Never allow a provider whose terms nobody opened.** The switch does not operate, the reason is
  on the row, and the check is on the server.
- **Never present a training promise as a switch.** Section 30 says it is a claim about the list,
  not a value. It is a locked row on S37 and it changes when this list changes.
- **Never probe a provider to fill the pool column.** A probe spends the pool it is reporting on.
- **Never show a provider's own number when we cannot read it.** Two of the four cannot report a
  remaining pool at all.
- **Never type a cost.** Compute it, or show nothing.
- **Never make the chain reorderable only by drag.** A keyboard path is required, because this screen
  is operated by exactly two people and one of them may be on a laptop trackpad on a train.
- **Never let the screen cap the model count.** The plan names fourteen and the founders asked for
  room for ten to thirteen. A layout that fits three is the defect.
- **Never save a chain of zero.** That is an outage configured by hand.

## Open questions

id | question | why it is open
`D87` | Is SambaNova in the chain? | Section 14 says yes, quoting 20 requests a day per model. The free-provider research lists it among those it never reached, `docs/research/2026-09-18-llm/raw/L1-free-providers.md:908`. The same open question is `D70` on S32
`D88` | Are per-model switches needed, or only per-provider? | SambaNova's limit is per model, and Groq serves four. A provider switch alone cannot express turning off one model that is misbehaving
`D89` | Who may record a provider's terms? | The record is what unlocks a switch, so whoever may write it effectively holds the sign-in promise. Section 30 says super admin holds the panel, and does not separate this
`D90` | Does the desktop's local model block belong here or in S37? | It is a routing choice, which argues for here, and it is also a capability that is on or off, which argues for a flag
