# What is not solid, and what would make it solid

A self-critique is the first opinion reviewing itself (LR#60). It is offered as the list of things to attack, not as a clean bill. Every item names what closes it.

## A. Verdict

The market and complaint mining is done to a high standard: 280 findings across two adversarial rounds, every load-bearing number re-opened by a second agent, and three of our own published numbers withdrawn when they failed a control. The product direction has moved four times in two days on that evidence (bytes → authorship → answer in place → review state), which is the process working, not failing.

What is not solid: the current direction has never itself been attacked; the form factor was never weighed; no human has been asked anything; the fundamentals and legal research that exist in the record were lost in compaction and are absent from the plan; the product name collides with an installed extension in the marketplace the buyer lives in; and the engineering estimates are unmethodical.

## D. Not reasoned properly — decisions the plan made without weighing the alternative

**D1. Form factor: standalone editor, or an extension inside the editor the buyer already uses.** The plan builds a full markdown editor (four modes, tree, tabs, search, palette) to deliver review state, because "editors ship the editor first". But the differentiator is not editing. The buyer measured in the VS Code round lives in VS Code: 78.06M Copilot Chat installs, 24.89M Claude Code extension installs, 14.45M markdown-all-in-one installs with a 10.6× co-recommendation lift alongside Copilot. A VS Code extension that tints changed-since-reviewed spans in `.md` files and keeps the sidecar would meet that population with none of the table-stakes cost the audits called larger than the novel-feature gap. It would also forfeit the free-editor distribution story and the Obsidian audience. **Never weighed. This is the largest unreasoned decision in the plan.**

**D2. The name.** The record (line 4827) notes that "Front Matter CMS" is an existing VS Code extension with about 80,605 installs. The plan never mentions it. If D1 resolves toward VS Code, the product name is taken in the exact store it would ship to; even if it does not, search and word of mouth collide. **Untreated.**

**D3. Generation versus "we are not the generator".** F5 and F6 ship in MVP-0 on founder evidence, while §1 says the generators are free inside the agent and frontmatter is the reading surface. The reconciliation offered — generation inside the review loop — is plausible but points at a buyer who does *not* run an agent (the non-developer, the liable person), which contradicts decision 2's recommendation to start with the developer. The two founder-validated features and the recommended buyer pull in opposite directions and the plan does not say so.

**D4. What Pro sells at MVP-1.** Sync is MVP-2 and history is git. Pro at MVP-1 is shared review state, comments and MCP. The first is what GitHub Team bundles at $4; the second and third are untested. The tier is a price with a thin offer behind it, and the plan calls it a hypothesis without proposing what would make it thick (candidates: gates in CI pulled forward; a hosted read-only viewer if decision 3 goes that way; the services engagement itself).

**D5. The consulting funnel.** §19 makes the repo docs scan the diagnostic for a Zephyrus engagement. Untested whether any team that runs the scan wants an engagement, and the record has no data on the studio's conversion from any productised diagnostic. It is a design, presented with the confidence of a finding.

**D6. Channels assumed cooperative.** "A README line in superpowers and spec-kit" assumes maintainers accept a PR that advertises a third-party editor. No approach has been made; both are outbound acts needing approval, and the answer may be no.

**D7. Export decided on the wrong population.** Decks and sites were cut using Obsidian plugin-download ratios, which measure note-takers. The refuter noted developers deploy from repos at scale (443,392 root CNAME files, 113,536 astro configs). The cut may be right for the wrong reason.

**D8. The Chrome extension's value.** "Open any `.md` URL in frontmatter" — GitHub already renders markdown. What the extension adds (review state on a file you do not own? the tint on someone else's README?) was never stated.

## F. The product questions, answered with confidence

Confidence: **E** evidence-backed (primary sources, verified twice) · **F** founder-reported · **R** reasoning · **U** untested.

**What the product should have.** Review state — tint, revert, panel, sidecar (E for the pain, U for the purchase). The editor's table stakes if standalone (E). Bring-your-own key and a hide-all-AI switch (E). Attribution shown when a write carries it (E). Answer in place (E, small). Idea mode and the decision flow (F). Repo docs scan (F for direction; the lint incumbents are E). Export, later (E). Sync, later (E).

**What it should not have.** Generation as the product (E), a kickoff prompt or vendor CDN (E), a local model now (E), plugins (E, a deliberate refusal), hosting (E), a chat sidebar (E), bookmarks (E), authorship as the headline (E), an AI panel open on load (E), a commercial licence as a feature (E).

**What approach is right.** Files stay in the user's repo (E: the architecture removes most legal surface; DPDP still applies to identity). Refuse rather than guess (E: measured against fuzzy patching). Their key, their agent, their machine (E: cost and trust). Agent-agnostic (E: the toolchains are). One sidecar, one engine, plain text (R). Test for two weeks before building for ten (E: three headlines refuted before a line was written).

**What approach is wrong.** Competing with free, agent-resident toolchains on generation (E). Pricing below the category's credibility floor, or charging for what GitHub bundles (E). Deciding the form factor without weighing the extension (R, D1). Treating complaint counts as demand (E: the graveyard baseline correction). Publishing a number without its denominator (E: three withdrawals).

**What will make it sell.** Hypotheses, all U: "see what the agent changed that you did not ask for" as the entry line; the review counter as the daily reason to return; a free editor that reads the folder you already have; a services engagement sold by the scan. Evidence-backed only in the negative: authorship marks did not sell (E), byte-exactness by name did not (E), decks are not asked for (E), standalone PRD generators have no traction (E).

**What will make it not sell.** Asking for an account before value (E, category convention). Any friction for the developer buyer (E, the audits). A second editor with nothing the first lacks (R). A name that is already an extension in their store (E for the collision, R for the effect).

**Markdown format improvements.** None to syntax (E: the MDZ verdict; Djot exists as the improved-syntax path and adoption is the reason not to follow it — to be stated in the plan with numbers). Four engine-side improvements: certified rendering with per-construct expectations; byte-safe frontmatter (NF-1..4); the sidecar as the only thing added beside the file; the byte-to-UTF-16 map for non-English (E for the defects, R for the priority).

**Offline, and the user's own tokens.** Settled and evidence-backed: desktop app works offline for everything except the model; web app fetches repo bytes in the browser with the user's token (E: api.github.com supports CORS); local folder without install only on desktop Chrome and Edge (E: caniuse 2026-09-08); AI on the user's key from their machine, or through their agent over MCP (U whether agents route writes through us); no local model (E: fabrication under constrained decoding).

**Helping the user derive and define.** Define: idea mode produces the PRD, FRD, BRD, note or spec (F). Derive: the decision flow asks what the idea leaves open and writes the answers into frontmatter (F); the repo docs scan derives stale sections, broken links and missing documents from git dates and the tree (F, no model); answer in place closes the questions an agent left (E, small). Templates for the document types that recur — spec, decision record, handover, changelog — with the canon's actual standards named and the absence of a PRD standard stated (E).

## G. What makes it solid, in order

1. **Attack the current direction.** One adversarial round on "review state is the product", with the same discipline the previous three headlines got. Until then the plan's page one is a hypothesis with a good pedigree. (One workflow; needs the go.)
2. **Decide the form factor with numbers.** Standalone editor vs VS Code extension vs both: population reached, table-stakes cost, distribution, the name collision. (Half a day of reasoning on data already in hand, then the founders.)
3. **Resolve the name.** Front Matter CMS holds "Front Matter" in the VS Code Marketplace. Keep, rename, or scope the name to the desktop product. (Founders.)
4. **Attach the founder evidence.** The F5/F6/F7 user-test notes, or relabel them "untested". (Founders.)
5. **Run the tools we are targeting.** spec-kit, superpowers, Kiro, plan mode, on a scratch repo; record what a human sees afterwards. (Half a day.)
6. **Spike the plugin.** Can a community plugin fix nested live preview? If not, the week-2 channel is gone and §12/§18 change. (One day.)
7. **Ask the sgnk-md users.** If they exist, they are the interviews. (Founders.)
8. **Restore the fundamentals to the plan.** The dialect landscape with Djot named, the CommonMark ambiguity catalogue the certificate needs, and DPDP in the operating rules. (Writing, from the record.)
9. **Replace the day estimates with a method.** Velocity from the last thirty days of commits on this repo, and the 2.5× correction the audits already found. (Half a day.)
10. **Write the measurement decision and the byte path.** Both are week-0 items and both are still open. (Founders, one page each.)
11. **Run the Mobbin benchmark in a fresh session.** Exhibit 22. (One session.)
12. **Then the two weeks of human tests**, as planned, with the three headline arms and the five-second screenshot test.

Items 1, 2, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11 can be done without the founders. Items 3, 4, 7, 10 cannot.
