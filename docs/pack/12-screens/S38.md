## Purpose

Find one account, see it against every limit, help that one person without moving the plan for
everybody else, and see what every setting change has been.

## Entry and exit

how a person arrives | route or control
A founder, from the panel nav | `/config/accounts`
See who, from S35's pending bar | The same route, filtered to the accounts a save would move over a cap
A support mail about one person | The same route, searched by address

how they leave | what happens
Granting an exception | Stays here. The account's meters redraw against its new limits
The panel nav | Another section
Clearing the search | The screen with no account chosen, which is its resting state

**This is the screen that makes a cap survivable.** Section 5b: an exception is the honest answer to
one support mail, and section 30 requires it to expire.

## Data contract

what | direction | port or store | shape | when
Account search | read | `AccountDirectoryReader.find(query)` | address, plan, joined, and a count per entitlement | On searching
Resolved limits | read | `LimitsReader.forAccount(accountId)` | the plan row plus any exception, the same function the product uses | On choosing an account
Usage | read | `UsageLedgerReader.currentPeriod(accountId)` | a count per entitlement id | On choosing an account
Cost | read | `UsageLedgerReader.spend(accountId)` | what the account cost us this period | On choosing an account
Ledger | read | `UsageLedgerReader.entries(accountId)` | when, what, which model, cost | On choosing an account
Exception | write | `ExceptionWriter.grant(accountId, entitlementId, value, expiry, reason)` | one exception | On granting
Exception | read | `LimitsReader.forAccount(accountId)` | the exception and its expiry, resolved into the limit set | Everywhere, through the one read path
Audit log | read | `ConfigAuditReader.all(limit, cursor)` | who, which setting, from, to, when, accounts moved | On opening

**The ledger is read here, not recounted.** Section 18 defines a ledger entry as account, kind,
delta, model and cost, kept 180 days and then aggregated. This screen is a view of those rows and
never a second tally. S29 shows the same person their own half of it.

**An exception resolves through the same function as a plan.** Section 30: `limitsFor(account)`
resolves a plan row **plus any exception** into the limit set. So an exception is not a bypass around
the cap check. It is an input to it, which is why granting one cannot create a path the ordinary
enforcement does not know about.

**Retention is set elsewhere and matters here.** Section 30 keeps the audit log as long as the
security log of section 23, which is 180 days rolling in Indian jurisdiction. This screen displays
it and never prunes it.

**`src/modules/config/` does not exist** at `0af3c90`, verified with `ls src/modules/`. Specified,
not built.

## Actions

action | trigger | what happens | failure behaviour | event id
Search for an account | Typing an address | The matched account, its plan and its counts | `E558` when nothing matches, said plainly and without guessing at a near match | `config.account.searched`
Open an account | Choosing a result | Its four meters, its ledger, and any exception it holds | `E651` when the ledger cannot be read. The meters still render, and the ledger area says why | `config.account.opened`
Grant an exception | The grant control | A dialogue asking which limit, what value, until when, and why. **The expiry is required** | `E046` when no expiry is given. The grant is refused, because an exception without one is a plan change for one person | `config.exception.granted`
Let an exception lapse | Time passing | The account returns to its plan. If it is now over a cap it meets S33: everything readable, nothing new created | None. Lapsing is not a failure | `config.exception.lapsed`
End an exception early | The control on the exception | The same as lapsing, immediately, and audited | `E106` when the write fails. The exception stands | `config.exception.ended`
Read the audit log | Opening the section | Every setting change, newest first, read-only | `E105` when it cannot be read. The rest of the screen is unaffected | `config.audit.viewed`
Change a plan for everyone | Not available here | **Nothing.** That is S35 | `E062` if attempted by any path | `config.change.blocked`

**An exception moves one person, never the plan.** That sentence is the screen's whole boundary, and
it is the difference between this screen and S35.

## Keyboard and focus

- Focus lands in the search field, because nothing on this screen happens before an account is
  chosen.
- Enter searches. Down moves into the results.
- The grant dialogue traps focus while it is open, because it is a real dialogue with a required
  field, and returns focus to the grant control on close.
- The ledger and the audit log are tables, navigated as S35's is.
- **No shortcut grants.** A grant changes what one person's product allows.

## Responsive

- The phone shows the search result and two meter cards rather than four. The generator halves the
  grid.
- The ledger and the audit log are not shown on the phone.
- Granting is not offered on the phone, for the same reason S35 does not edit there.

## What this screen must never do

- **Never move the plan.** One account at a time. S35 moves plans, and the two are separate screens
  so that the more dangerous one is harder to reach by accident.
- **Never grant an exception without an expiry.** Section 30 requires it. An exception that does not
  end is a plan row with one member and no record of why.
- **Never let an exception bypass the cap check.** It is an input to `limitsFor(account)`, not a
  route around it.
- **Never delete or edit an audit row.** Read-only in the panel, kept as long as the security log.
- **Never show a directory of every account.** The screen is for one person at a time, and a browsable
  list of everybody is a privacy surface nobody asked for.
- **Never guess at a near match.** Acting on the wrong account is the failure mode this screen has.
- **Never show more of a person's content than the counts and the ledger.** Their documents are
  theirs. This screen reports quantities, never text.
- **Never let a lapse remove anything.** It returns the account to its plan, which is S33's rule.

It warns when the expiry will put the account over a cap. Rejected: free-text keys
`D89` | Where does the audit log live, given the nav has a fifth entry? | `CFG_NAV` lists Audit log as its own section and this screen also draws it. Rejected: two separate logs
`D90` | How long may an exception run? | Section 30 requires an expiry and names no ceiling. An exception with a ten-year expiry satisfies the letter and defeats the point | **resolved (proposed 18 Sep, founder review).** At most 365 days, as `28-CONFIGURATION-PANEL-SPEC.md` section 7.1 already bounds `expires`.
