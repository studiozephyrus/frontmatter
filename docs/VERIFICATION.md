### 89.5 What this pass says about the record's reliability

**13 CONFIRMED of 34 sub-claims = 38.2%. 18 needed an edit (13 REVISED + 5 REFUTED) = 52.9%. 3 could not be checked at all = 8.8%.** `[derived]`

That ratio is the finding, and it should be read as an indictment of method rather than of care. A 38% clean rate means **the original research read summaries where it should have opened statutes, and read vendor marketing where it should have opened marketplace listings**. The pattern is visible in the shape of the errors, not just their count:

- **Where the source was a statute, a spec file, or a package registry, the record was right.** Rows 1, 2, 4, 9, 10, 13, 20 all came back CONFIRMED verbatim. CommonMark 0.31.2, `blocksToMarkdownLossy()`, IGST §16, the ₹5 crore e-invoicing threshold — every claim resting on a document with a canonical URL survived.
- **Where the source was a number quoted in prose, the record was wrong about half the time, and wrong in a consistent direction.** Cluster 6 named it explicitly: four of its nine sub-claims were directionally right and numerically wrong, **and all four erred in the direction that flattered the argument**. Same pattern elsewhere — "under 4%" understated 4.03%; "+120" understated $144; "~20%" understated +33%; "561 of 3,220" overstated a 33-comment finding by roughly seventy times.
- **Where the source was a vendor, the record inherited the vendor's framing.** The 40–60% AI-deflection band, the "$7,000–$30,000" compliance-platform range, and the 30–40% checkout-loss figure all came from parties selling the thing being measured. None survived contact with a marketplace listing or an independent study.
- **Three refutations were the record repeating a plausible-sounding structural claim nobody had checked**: that the IT Rules set the SSMI number (they defer it), that Art. 27 is "often cited as Art. 13" (two different laws), that international gateways lack UPI (Stripe serves 35 countries with it, just not India). These are the most dangerous class, because they are not arithmetic slips — they are *architecture* the record was building on.

The implication for the claims **not** in this batch is direct and unflattering: **any figure in the record that supports a thesis and was not opened in this pass should be treated as unverified until it is.** The 38% base rate is the prior. Apply the sorting rule the pass itself produced — statutes, specs and registries are probably fine; **numbers quoted in prose, vendor-published percentages, and any absence claim ("nobody has measured…", "no tool does…") are the three categories where this pass found nearly every error.**

Two structural upgrades came out of it and should be treated as permanent:

1. **Two absence claims got floors under them.** "India's markdown community is greenfield" is now `[measured]` — 440 pushers vs 1,296, and six years of the `obsidian` topic never clearing GitHub's 100-developer reporting floor for India. "Sync silently destroys data" is now `[measured]` at 445/13,731. Both are falsifiable and both should be re-run on a schedule rather than restated.
2. **The strongest surviving evidence for Principle #3 is not a name, it is a confession.** `blocksToMarkdownLossy()` was CONFIRMED, but the load-bearing artefact is the doc comment beside it: a competitor stating in its own shipped source that Markdown cannot carry its structure, so children get un-nested and styles get dropped. Quote the comment, cite `@blocknote/core@0.54.0, src/editor/BlockNoteEditor.ts:1308`, and retire the bare name.
