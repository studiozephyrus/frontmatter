### The India threshold is an executive dial, not a statutory one

Rule 2(1)(v) is a pointer, and S.O. 942(E) is the value it points at [fetched, 2026-08-31]. Nothing in the Rules constrains the number — no floor, no notice period, no parliamentary step. **The 50-lakh line can move by a one-page gazette notification signed by an Additional Secretary**, which is exactly how it was set (Dr. Rajendra Kumar, Addl. Secy., F. No. 16(4)/2020-CLES). For a product with a sync backend and a public share surface, the planning assumption is not "we are 50 lakh users away from SSMI duties" but "the SSMI trigger is a number a ministry can change in a week" [inference].

The duties that attach on crossing it are staffing duties, not engineering duties: a Chief Compliance Officer, a 24×7 Nodal Contact Person, and a Resident Grievance Officer — **all three resident in India** — plus a monthly compliance report and a published physical address in India [fetched, pib.gov.in PRID 1700749, read 2026-08-31]. For an India-based founder the residency requirement is free; the three-headcount requirement is not.

Prighter page dated 2026-08-24, EDPO price object read live; both read 2026-08-31 [fetched]. **The €200–500/month band in the record is the 51–500-employee price. A one-person company pays €39–€160/month.** Budget the top of that range (€160/mo ≈ €1,920/yr) rather than the bottom, because the cheap tiers are usage-capped and a public-facing editor draws data-subject requests.

Two further corrections that change the shape of the obligation, not just the price:

- **GDPR Art. 27 has an exemption the record does not mention.** Art. 27(2)(a) removes the duty where processing "is occasional, does not include, on a large scale, processing of special categories of data … and is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons" [fetched, EUR-Lex CELEX:32016R0679, read 2026-08-31]. A subscription editor with EU accounts and a sync backend is **not** occasional processing, so the exemption almost certainly does not apply here — but the record should say why it does not apply rather than omitting that it exists [inference].
- **The EU Accessibility Act carries a microenterprise exemption that the record needs before it budgets for WCAG conformance.** Directive (EU) 2019/882 Art. 4(5): "**Microenterprises providing services shall be exempt from complying with the accessibility requirements** referred to in paragraph 3 of this Article and any obligations relating to the compliance with those requirements." Art. 3(23) defines microenterprise as **fewer than 10 persons and turnover or balance sheet ≤ €2 million**. Products placed on the market after 28 June 2025 are in scope [fetched, EUR-Lex CELEX:32019L0882, read 2026-08-31]. A one-founder company is squarely inside the exemption **today, and exits it on the tenth hire or the €2m line, whichever comes first** [inference].

### axe coverage: the denominator is the finding

Three independent measurements agree once you fix the denominator [fetched + measured, all read 2026-08-31]:

| Denominator | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| WCAG 2.1 AA success criteria with any automated issue | **16 / 50 = 32.0%** `[derived]` | Deque coverage report |
| Distinct failure types on the GDS synthetic corpus, axe errors only | **41 / 142 = 28.9%** `[measured]` | alphagov `tests.json`, computed here |
| Same corpus, axe errors + manual prompts | **43 / 142 = 30.3%** `[measured]` | same |
| Volume of real issues across 13,000 audited pages | **57.38%** `[fetched]` | Deque coverage report / axe-core README |
| axe DevTools suite incl. Intelligent Guided Tests (marketing) | "up to 80%" `[fetched]` | deque.com/axe |

**"Roughly a third" is defensible and "57%" is defensible; they are not in conflict, and the record must not pick one without naming its denominator.** Deque's report says so itself, in terms: the success-criteria measure "supports the 20 to 30% automated coverage claims that many experts claim today," and their argument is that this measure understates value, not that it is arithmetically wrong. The honest sentence for a certification-selling product is that **automation clears about a third of the distinct WCAG failure modes and about 57% of the issue count, and 12.7% of failure types on the GDS corpus are found by none of the 13 tools tested** [measured] — which is the argument for a refuse-rather-than-guess engine, not against it.

One caveat that must travel with the GDS numbers: the corpus is a synthetic worst-case page (142 seeded failure types with tool results, one instance each), so it measures *breadth of rule coverage*, not what a real document triggers, and the repo has drifted from the 2017 blog post that describes it (blog: 143 barriers, 42 missed by all 10 tools = 29%; current `master`: 142 cases with results, 18 missed by all 13 tools = 12.7%) [measured, read 2026-08-31]. Cite the dataset, not the blog post.
